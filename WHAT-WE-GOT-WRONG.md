# What we got wrong

This explainer was first built in July 2026, taken down in October (it did not fit a site then built around AI), and brought back as an experiment on 25 October 2026. Before it went back up, an independent fact-checker re-checked every number against its source. These are the errors it found, and a few the final review found in the page itself.

## Claims

**The ladder explanation was backwards.** Mishra's article prints a ladder: 9% dollar growth per person crosses the high-income line in 2047, 8% in 2051, 7% in 2055, 6% in 2062. The calculator gets 2047, 2051, 2056 and 2063. The first version blamed the gap on the article's use of a shortcut (adding growth rates instead of multiplying them). But the exact formula gives *faster* growth, which would pull dates earlier, not later. The real cause: worked backwards, the four rungs imply four different starting incomes, from about $3,170 to $3,370. No single start reproduces all four. *Lesson: when you explain a discrepancy, check the sign of your explanation.*

**"IMF estimates for 2025 sit near $2,900."** The IMF's April 2026 estimate is about $2,675. With that start, the 9% path crosses in 2050, not 2049.

**"85 economies were high-income in 2024."** The article says "at least 85". The World Bank's July 2026 list has 87.

**India's rank, and why it improved.** The World Bank revised its data on 8 October 2026, which moved Vietnam from #129 to #130 and India from #154 to #155 for 2024. More important: ten economies that had 2020 data have no 2024 figure yet, and nine of them were richer than India. About nine of India's 14-place gain from 2020 to 2024 comes from them dropping out of the sample, not from India overtaking anyone. *Lesson: a rank is a claim about a sample. Check who is in it each year.*

**"IMF-tracked nations on 2025 estimates."** The article says only "ranked 140 globally among 196 nations". That it used IMF figures for 2025 is our inference, and the page now says so.

**The median-age year.** The text said the article projects India's median age crossing 40 around 2053; the calculator shaded the chart from 2050. Now both say 2053.

## The page

**The threshold box refused the number the text told readers to try.** The text suggested starting from the World Bank's actual line, $14,375. The input box only accepted steps of $250, so it would not take 14,375.

**Three of four tabs were invisible without JavaScript.** The house rule is that a page must be complete with JavaScript off. Now all four show, and the script hides three only once it is running.
